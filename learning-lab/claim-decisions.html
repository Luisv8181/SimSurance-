A claim is a question, not a payment.

A provider submits information to request payment. The system then checks the information against applicable requirements. Some claims are paid, some denied, and some need more review. These example rules are invented for learning only.

What should a careful system do?

1
Check before concluding.
Missing enrollment or provider information is not the same as proof that coverage does not exist.
2
Distinguish the status.
Paid, denied, rejected, pending, and needs-review should not be treated as interchangeable labels.
3
Show the reasons.
A result should identify the inputs and source-backed rules used. If no applicable rule is verified, the model should remain unresolved.
4
Keep human rights in view.
Actual decisions may have notice, correction, grievance, or appeal pathways. The responsible official materials determine what applies.

What is source-backed in this lesson?

Nothing about the outcome of the selectable claim scenarios represents an actual Pennsylvania rule. The lesson demonstrates the product principle: missing or conflicting information should be visible, and the system should not invent a policy answer.
