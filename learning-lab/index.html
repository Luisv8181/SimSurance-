Case file 01

Jordan can get coverage. Can Jordan get care?

Jordan is a fictional 16-year-old seeking help with anxiety. In this learning scenario, Jordan has Medicaid. A provider appears in the directory, but the clinic says the next opening is several weeks away.

What do we know?

Jordan is described as covered in the scenario; a provider appears in a directory; the provider reports no immediate opening.

What is still unknown?

Is the directory current? Does the provider participate in Jordan's specific plan for this service? Is the service covered in these circumstances? Are other appropriate providers available? Are language, transport, scheduling, or accessibility needs relevant?

What does the metaphor teach?

A provider directory is like a map to a possible destination. It does not guarantee the destination is open, suitable, or reachable today.

Case file 03 · Practice operations

Why does a private practice accept some insurance plans and not others?

A payer decision is a business, clinical-access, and contract decision. Practices usually weigh the likely benefit to clients against payment, eligibility requirements, workload, and risk. There is no single universal rule that every practice follows.

1. Client demand

Which plans do current and prospective clients have? Which communities does the practice aim to serve? Would joining the plan help people get access to appropriate care?

2. Net revenue, not just rate

Compare the contracted payment with clinician compensation, billing and collection time, documentation, denials, cancellations, payment delays, and other costs. The highest session rate may not yield the best net return.

3. Credentialing and eligibility

Check license and provider-type rules, credentialing, enrollment, malpractice requirements, group versus individual contracting, and whether the payer is accepting providers in that network.

4. Contract and administration

Review covered services, billing codes, authorizations, referrals, timely filing, audits, recoupments, appeals, documentation, and the resources needed to follow the rules.

5. Strategic fit

Consider referral flow, waitlists, clinician capacity, continuity, mission, payer concentration, and whether the contract supports a sustainable practice. A practice may apply, renew, decline, or reconsider participation subject to its contract and applicable law.

6. Recheck after joining

Track claims submitted and paid, denial reasons, days to payment, staff time per claim, completed visits, client access, and contract changes. Use aggregate, de-identified information for learning.

Pennsylvania's extra Medicaid distinction

For Behavioral HealthChoices, distinguish Medicaid enrollment from participation in a specific county's Behavioral Health Managed Care Organization (BH-MCO) network. The state identifies the BH-MCO by member county, and provider participation/network status must be checked separately. Physical HealthChoices plans are not automatically the behavioral-health payer.

Knowledge check: a useful first question

Questions to ask a practice owner or biller

  1. Which exact payer products do we participate in, and are contracts held by the group, individual clinicians, or both?
  2. How are rates, denial patterns, payment delays, and administrative hours reviewed?
  3. How do we know a clinician, service, and client plan are all in-network and eligible for the intended billing arrangement?
  4. What would make us join, renew, or discontinue a network, and what contract notice requirements apply?

Use aggregate, de-identified data. Do not enter client-identifying health or billing details into this learning exercise.

Why can someone have Medicaid and still struggle to find a therapist?

Coverage is one step in a longer path. Use the case to trace where a person might get stuck, what the state says, and what evidence you would need before blaming a particular organization.

Follow the pathway

1. Coverage

The person is enrolled in Medicaid. That is the starting point, not proof that a particular therapist has an open appointment.

Question: Is the person's coverage active?

2. County / BH-MCO

Pennsylvania says the behavioral-health managed-care organization is assigned based on county of residence.

Question: Which BH-MCO serves this county today?

3. Provider participation

The member needs a provider who participates in the relevant network and offers the service they need.

Question: Is this exact clinician and service in network?

4. Appointment capacity

A directory listing alone does not prove the provider is accepting new clients or can offer a timely appointment.

What Pennsylvania DHS says

“Each HealthChoices consumer is assigned a Behavioral Health Managed Care Organization (BH-MCO) based on his or her county of residence.”

Source: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Behavioral Health Managed Care Organizations (BH-MCOs). The quote describes assignment by county; it does not establish a specific person's enrollment, a provider's current network status, or appointment availability.

“If you’re having trouble finding a provider, contact your BH-MCO.”

Source: Pennsylvania DHS, Request Behavioral HealthChoices Program Services. This is member-facing guidance about a next step, not a guarantee that a particular provider or appointment will be available.

Try it: what would you investigate first?

A member says, “I have Medicaid, but three therapists I called said they cannot see me.” Choose the most defensible next step.

The correct investigation does not assume the cause. The barrier might involve network participation, availability, service needs, inaccurate directory information, or other case-specific factors. Each requires evidence.

Member path and provider / advocate path

If you are seeking care

  1. Confirm the county-based BH-MCO.
  2. Use the BH-MCO's current provider directory and call to confirm availability.
  3. If you cannot find care, contact the BH-MCO and document dates, responses, and unresolved barriers.
  4. Use the county mental-health office or the state service guidance when additional navigation is needed.

If you are a provider or advocate

  1. Identify the exact program, county, BH-MCO, service, and provider type.
  2. Check the current network and applicable contract / provider manual.
  3. Record what happened without identifiable client information in the research notes.
  4. Separate a documented access problem from a theory about its cause.

Case file 02

A budget isn't a waiting room.

A fictional behavioral-health organization has an annual budget for defined services, but clinicians are booked and several positions are vacant.

What might additional resources support?

Recruitment, retention, supervision, space, technology, or other operating needs. Each option takes time and depends on workforce availability and organizational decisions.

Why might access not improve immediately?

Funding and appointment capacity are different resources. Available qualified clinicians, schedules, geography, and the needs of the people seeking care all matter.

What should we measure?

For a real evaluation, define measures such as wait time, appointments offered, completed visits, continuity, workforce vacancies, and unmet need. A simulation should label assumptions and uncertainty rather than promise an outcome.

Small dictionary

Insurance words in human language.

Start here when a term appears in a lesson. These short definitions are educational; actual policy meanings must be checked in the relevant source.

Eligibility

Whether a person meets a program's requirements at a particular time. It does not automatically answer whether a particular service is authorized or a claim will be paid.

Provider network

The providers or organizations recognized by a plan under specified terms. A network listing does not guarantee an open appointment.

Claim

A structured request for payment for a service. A claim is not itself a payment and may need review.

Allowed amount

The amount recognized under applicable payment terms for a service. The calculation depends on the relevant contract, fee schedule, benefit, and circumstances.

Capitation

A prospective payment for a defined population, service scope, and period, under contract terms. It differs from paying a clinic for each individual service.

Access to care

The practical ability to find, reach, and receive appropriate care. Coverage matters, but so do capacity, transportation, language access, scheduling, and accommodations.
