Interactive lesson / 06 · Source literacy & provenance

How to read a policy source without over-claiming.

When people argue about insurance coverage or reimbursement, they often cite a web page, a summary chart, or an old contract as if it answered every question. In policy research and software modeling, a source only proves what its specific version, effective date, section, and applicability establish.

Step 1 · Real-world question

"Does this official page prove my claim should be paid at $X?"

Imagine you find an official state webpage stating that a behavioral-health program covers outpatient therapy. Can you encode that webpage directly into a billing or simulation rule that pays every outpatient therapy claim? No. A high-level program description tells you the program exists and who oversees it—not the contract rate, prior-authorization rule, provider network status, or effective date for a specific service.

Step 2 · Plain-language explanation

The six fields every policy citation needs

Before SimSurance converts any policy statement into code or teaches it as a verified rule, our Source Register requires six distinct checks:

01 · Title & Publisher

Who issued this document?

Distinguish a state agency (e.g., PA DHS), a managed care organization (BH-MCO), a federal regulator (CMS), and a third-party blog.

02 · Version & Effective Date

When did this rule govern?

A 2026 Program Standards & Requirements (PS&R) document does not automatically match a 2024 claim or a later contract amendment.

03 · Exact Section / Location

Where can a human check it?

Record the exact URL, PDF page, section number, or appendix so another researcher can verify the wording without guessing.

04 · Applicability Scope

Who and what does it cover?

Specify whether the text applies to Behavioral HealthChoices, Physical HealthChoices, commercial ACA plans, a specific county, or a specific provider type.

05 · Exact Statement

What does the text actually say?

Summarize or quote only what is written. Do not extrapolate a statewide payment rate from a general program description.

06 · What Remains Unknown

What does it NOT establish?

Explicitly list the missing pieces (such as individual provider contracts, fee schedules, or clinical medical-necessity criteria).
