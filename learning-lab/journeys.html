Compare journeys · 6–8 minutes

One system.
Different journeys.

Choose a fictional person's situation, follow the decisions, then switch to another journey. The point is to notice what changes, what stays the same, and what evidence is still missing.

All people and practice details below are fictional. These scenarios teach reasoning; they do not determine actual coverage or payment.

JOURNEY 01 · MEMBER ACCESS

Can Jordan get care?

Jordan is a fictional teenager with Medicaid who wants help with anxiety. A directory lists a therapist, but the clinic reports no opening for six weeks.

01 · Known

Medicaid coverage is reported in the scenario.

02 · Check

Verify the applicable county-based behavioral-health plan and provider participation.

03 · Unknown

Whether the listing is current and whether suitable appointments are available sooner.

04 · Next step

Contact the plan and check current network and access options.

Which conclusion is best supported?

Transfer question: Which facts are confirmed, which are assumptions, and which source would you check next?

Compare what changed

After exploring at least two journeys, use this comparison to avoid assuming one explanation fits every problem.

Person's goal

Find appropriate care soon.

Main decision

What to verify before concluding the plan failed or the service is uncovered.

Missing evidence

Current network status, plan assignment, and actual availability.

Why this matters

The same system contains different decision points. A member is trying to access care; a practice is deciding whether it can responsibly join a plan; a claims reviewer is checking a particular transaction against applicable rules. They need different evidence, even though their journeys affect one another.
