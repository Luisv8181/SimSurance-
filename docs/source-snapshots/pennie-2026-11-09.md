## Finding for the evidence model
A homepage that held still for three days has now moved — not by rotating
its hero, but by adding a disclosure at its foot. For the modeler, the
lesson is three-sided. First, an "unchanged" streak ends the way it
always does on a live page: with a change, and the change here is the
kind a re-check exists to catch — a new public fact, not a rewording.
Second, read the notice, not just the headline: the homepage line says
"a data security incident at one of our vendors," and only the linked
PDF states which vendor, what was involved, what was not, and when
Pennie learned of it — a one-line summary is not the evidence. Third,
keep the dates separate: incident discovery (January 2026), Pennie's
notification (October 27, 2026), and the homepage notice's first
appearance (November 9, 2026) are three different dates, and conflating
any two misstates the record. Companion workbook scene
u1_p5c5_pennie_incident (shipped this run) teaches the discipline: quote
the disclosure's scope exactly, and date every layer of it. The
November 6 scene u1_p5c4_pennie_steady's quoted lines remain accurate
evidence of 2026-11-06; they must still not be re-cited as the page's
current text after this rotation.
