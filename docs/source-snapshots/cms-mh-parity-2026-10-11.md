## What the page states (page-level verification only; no rules encoded)

1. MHPAEA (the Paul Wellstone and Pete Domenici Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act of 2008) "generally prevents group health plans and health insurance issuers that provide mental health or substance use disorder (MH/SUD) benefits from imposing less favorable benefit limitations on those benefits than on medical/surgical benefits."
2. Financial requirements (such as coinsurance and copays) and treatment limitations (such as visit limits) on MH/SUD benefits cannot be more restrictive than the predominant financial requirements and treatment limitations applying to substantially all medical/surgical benefits in a classification. Separate requirements/limitations that apply only to MH/SUD benefits are prohibited. Disclosure requirements also apply.
3. The law does NOT require plans to cover MH/SUD benefits. The ACA requires MH/SUD services as one of ten essential health benefit (EHB) categories in non-grandfathered individual and small group plans.
4. The 2013 final regulation applies the substantially all / predominant test separately to six classifications: inpatient in-network, inpatient out-of-network, outpatient in-network, outpatient out-of-network, emergency, and prescription drugs. Plans that cover MH/SUD benefits must do so in all classifications in which medical/surgical benefits are provided.
5. Quantitative treatment limitations are numerical (visit limits, day limits). Nonquantitative treatment limitations (NQTLs) include medical management, step therapy, pre-authorization / prior authorization; scope and duration limits such as geographic limits, facility-type limits, and network adequacy are subject to NQTL parity requirements. NQTLs on MH/SUD benefits must use processes, strategies, evidentiary standards, or other factors comparable to, and applied no more stringently than, those used for medical/surgical benefits in the classification.
6. September 2024 final rules: reinforce NQTL parity (examples: prior authorization and medical management techniques, network composition standards, methodologies to determine out-of-network reimbursement rates); require plans/issuers to collect and evaluate data and act on material differences in access; require comparative analyses of NQTL design and application (per CAA 2021); prohibit discriminatory information/evidence/sources/standards.
7. Applicability: MHPAEA originally applied to group plans and group coverage; the ACA extended it to individual health insurance coverage. ERISA/Code small employers (50 or fewer employees) are generally exempt; some states have stricter requirements. Medicare, Medicaid, and CHIP are not group health plans or issuers, but Social Security Act provisions require Medicaid MCOs, CHIP plans, and alternative benefit (benchmark) plans to comply with certain MHPAEA requirements.
8. Enforcement contacts: CMS help line 1-877-267-2323 ext. 6-1565 / phig@cms.hhs.gov; DOL benefit advisors via askebsa.dol.gov or 1-866-444-3272.
