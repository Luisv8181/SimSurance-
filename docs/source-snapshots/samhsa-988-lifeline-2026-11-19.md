## What the pages state (official-site claims only)
SAMHSA page (https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/988):
- "988 offers 24/7 judgment-free support for mental health, substance use, and more. Text, call, or chat 988. Access for Deaf/HoH and Spanish speakers."
- "Providing 24/7, free and confidential support to people in suicidal crisis or emotional distress works."
- "In 2020, Congress designated the new 988 dialing code to be operated through the existing National Suicide Prevention Lifeline. SAMHSA sees 988 as a first step towards a transformed crisis care system in America."
- Header: "In Crisis? Call or Text 988".
- The page is framed for states, territories, tribes, and professionals: "The resources and information on this page are designed to help states, territories, tribes, mental health and substance use disorder professionals, and others looking for information on understanding the background, history, funding opportunities, and implementation resources for strengthening suicide prevention and mental health crisis services."

988 Lifeline homepage (https://988lifeline.org/):
- "The 988 Lifeline is available 24/7/365. Your conversations are free and confidential."
- "Call, text, or chat with a 988 Lifeline counselor for help during difficult moments anytime, day or night."
- "The 988 Lifeline is for everyone. Through the 988 Lifeline, you have access to free, quality, one-on-one assistance."

## What the pages do NOT state (limits recorded, not filled in)
- The SAMHSA page does NOT state in so many words that SAMHSA itself administers or operates the Lifeline. The operator named by the page's own history sentence is the existing National Suicide Prevention Lifeline, through which Congress designated the 988 code to be operated. Do not write "SAMHSA operates 988" as a verified claim.
- Neither page states any eligibility rule, coverage rule, benefit, rate, county routing, wait-time figure, or call-volume figure. Nothing on either page is a Medicaid rule or a simulation parameter.
- The 988 Lifeline homepage carries no revision date or last-updated stamp in the fetched text; the SAMHSA page's stamp (09/26/2025) is the only dateable fact besides the retrieval date.
