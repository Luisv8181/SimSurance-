## What the page states (official-site claims only)
- MHPAEA (Paul Wellstone and Pete Domenici Act of 2008) "generally prevents
  group health plans and health insurance issuers that provide mental health
  or substance use disorder (MH/SUD) benefits from imposing less favorable
  benefit limitations on those benefits than on medical/surgical benefits."
  It "preserves the MHPA protections and added significant new protections,
  such as extending the parity requirements to substance use disorders."
- Financial requirements (coinsurance, copays) and treatment limitations
  (visit limits) on MH/SUD benefits "cannot be more restrictive than the
  predominant financial requirements and treatment limitations that apply to
  substantially all medical/surgical benefits in a classification." Separate
  MH/SUD-only financial requirements/treatment limitations are prohibited.
  Disclosure requirements also apply.
- Key caveat: "MHPAEA does NOT require group health plans or health
  insurance issuers to cover MH/SUD benefits." The ACA (as amended) requires
  MH/SUD coverage as one of ten essential health benefit (EHB) categories in
  non-grandfathered individual and small group plans.
- 2013 final regulation (effective 2014-01-13; plan years on/after
  2014-07-01): the substantially-all/predominant test is applied separately
  to six classifications: inpatient in-network; inpatient out-of-network;
  outpatient in-network; outpatient out-of-network; emergency; and
  prescription drug. Sub-classifications permitted for office visits vs other
  outpatient services, and for multi-tier in-network providers. Cumulative
  financial requirements (deductibles, out-of-pocket limits) must combine
  medical/surgical and MH/SUD benefits within a classification.
- QTL vs NQTL distinction: "Quantitative treatment limitations are numerical,
  such as visit limits and day limits. Nonquantitative treatment limitations
  include but are not limited to medical management, step therapy and
  pre-authorization." A plan cannot impose an NQTL on MH/SUD benefits unless
  the processes, strategies, evidentiary standards, or other factors used
  are "comparable to, and are applied no more stringently than" those used
  for medical/surgical benefits in the classification. All standards limiting
  scope/duration of benefits (geographic limits, facility-type limits,
  network adequacy) are subject to NQTL parity.
- CAA, 2021 (enacted 2020-12-27, §203 Title II Div BB): plans/issuers offering
  both benefit types and imposing NQTLs on MH/SUD benefits must "perform and
  document their comparative analyses of the design and application of
  NQTLs" and make them available to the Departments or State authorities on
  request (beginning 45 days after enactment).
- 2024 final rules (released 2024-09-09 by HHS/Labor/Treasury): NQTL examples
  listed as "prior authorization requirements and other medical management
  techniques, standards related to network composition, and methodologies to
  determine out-of-network reimbursement rates." Plans must "collect and
  evaluate data and take reasonable action, as necessary, to address material
  differences in access to MH/SUD benefits" attributable to NQTLs; may not
  use "discriminatory information, evidence, sources, or standards that
  systematically disfavor" MH/SUD access when designing NQTLs; sunset the
  self-funded non-Federal governmental plan opt-out elections.
- Applicability: MHPAEA originally applied to group plans/coverage; ACA
  extended it to individual coverage. Does not apply directly to small group
  plans (indirectly via ACA EHB). Medicaid MCOs, CHIP, and benchmark plans
  are "not group health plans" but "provisions of the Social Security Act ...
  require compliance with certain requirements of MHPAEA" — 2016 final rule
  linked for Medicaid MCOs/CHIP/Alternative Benefit Plans. Jurisdiction
  split: HHS over non-Federal governmental plans; DOL/Treasury over private
  sector group plans; state insurance departments regulate purchased
  insurance products. Some states may have stricter parity requirements.
