## What the page states (official-site claims only)
- MHPAEA (Paul Wellstone and Pete Domenici Act of 2008) "generally prevents
  group health plans and health insurance issuers that provide mental health
  or substance use disorder (MH/SUD) benefits from imposing less favorable
  benefit limitations on those benefits than on medical/surgical benefits."
- Financial requirements (coinsurance, copays) and treatment limitations
  (visit limits) on MH/SUD benefits "cannot be more restrictive than the
  predominant financial requirements and treatment limitations that apply to
  substantially all medical/surgical benefits in a classification." Separate
  MH/SUD-only financial requirements/treatment limitations are prohibited.
  Disclosure requirements also apply (page states they exist; it does not
  enumerate them here).
- NON-MANDATE (angle of the 2026-11-04 workbook scene): "MHPAEA does NOT
  require group health plans or health insurance issuers to cover MH/SUD
  benefits." The coverage backstop comes from elsewhere: the Affordable Care
  Act "requires coverage of mental health and substance use disorder
  services as one of ten essential health benefit (EHB) categories in
  non-grandfathered individual and small group plans."
- SMALL-GROUP APPLICABILITY: "MHPAEA does not apply directly to small group
  health plans, although its requirements are applied indirectly in
  connection with the Affordable Care Act's EHB requirements." Small employer
  generally means 1-50 employees, "with the option for states to expand the
  definition of small employer to 1-100 employees." The page adds: "Some
  states may have mental health parity requirements that are stricter than
  federal requirements."
- 2013 regulation applicability (unchanged): non-Federal governmental plans
  with more than 50 employees, private-employer group plans with more than
  50 employees, and individual-market coverage; not small-employer group
  plans except via EHB. The 2013 regulation "does not require group health
  plans to provide MH/SUD benefits" either.
- 2024 final rules (released September 9, 2024, HHS/Labor/Treasury): NQTL comparative-analysis content requirements and
  response timeframes; data collection and reasonable action on material
  differences in access; prohibition on discriminatory standards that
  systematically disfavor MH/SUD access; sunset of self-funded
  non-Federal governmental plan opt-out elections.
- HISTORY / TIMELINE (angle of the 2026-11-07 workbook scene): prior to
  MHPAEA, the Mental Health Parity Act of 1996 (MHPA) "provided that large
  group health plans cannot impose annual or lifetime dollar limits on
  mental health benefits that are less favorable than any such limits
  imposed on medical/surgical benefits." MHPAEA "preserves the MHPA
  protections and added significant new protections, such as extending the
  parity requirements to substance use disorders." The implementing
  regulation followed in two steps: an interim final regulation published
  February 2, 2010 (generally applying to plan years beginning on or after
  July 1, 2010), then a final regulation "published in the Federal Register
  on November 13, 2013. The regulation is effective January 13, 2014 and
  generally applies to plan years (in the individual market, policy years)
  beginning on or after July 1, 2014." Section 203 of Title II of Division
  BB of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (enacted December 27,
  2020) added the NQTL comparative-analysis duty: beginning 45 days after
  enactment, plans and issuers imposing NQTLs "must make their comparative
  analyses available to the Departments or applicable State authorities,
  upon request."
- HIDDEN-RULES TEST (angle of the 2026-11-10 workbook scene): the 2013
  regulation distinguishes quantitative treatment limitations, which "are
  numerical, such as visit limits and day limits," from nonquantitative
  treatment limitations, which "include but are not limited to medical
  management, step therapy and pre-authorization." A plan "cannot impose a
  nonquantitative treatment limitation with respect to MH/SUD benefits in
  any classification unless, under the terms of the plan (or coverage) as
  written and in operation, any processes, strategies, evidentiary standards,
  or other factors used in applying the nonquantitative treatment limitation
  to MH/SUD benefits in the classification are comparable to, and are
  applied no more stringently than, the processes, strategies, evidentiary
  standards, or other factors used in applying the limitation with respect
  to medical surgical/benefits in the classification." The final regulation
  "eliminated an exception that allowed for different nonquantitative
  treatment limitations 'to the extent that recognized clinically
  appropriate standards of care may permit a difference.'"
- 2024 DATA DUTY (angle of this run's new workbook scene): the September 9,
  2024 final rules "Require plans and issuers to collect and evaluate data
  and take reasonable action, as necessary, to address material differences
  in access to MH/SUD benefits as compared to medical/surgical benefits
  that result from application of NQTLs, where the relevant data suggest
  that the NQTL contributes to material differences in access." The same
  rules "Prohibit plans and issuers from using discriminatory information,
  evidence, sources, or standards that systematically disfavor or are
  specifically designed to disfavor access to MH/SUD benefits as compared to
  medical/surgical benefits when designing NQTLs," and "Implement the sunset
  provision for self-funded non-Federal governmental plan elections to opt
  out of compliance with MHPAEA."
