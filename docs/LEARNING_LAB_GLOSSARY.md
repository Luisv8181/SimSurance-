# Learning Lab Glossary

Plain-language explanations for education, not a substitute for applicable plan documents, contracts, or policy.

| Term | Plain-language explanation | Example | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medicaid | Public health coverage jointly funded by federal and state governments and administered under federal and state rules. | A person qualifies under applicable program rules. | Eligibility pathways and benefits vary. |
| Member | A person enrolled in a health plan or coverage program. | Someone seeking therapy. | This does not tell us what is covered in a specific case. |
| Provider | A person or organization delivering health care. | A licensed clinician or community clinic. | Qualifications and participation requirements depend on service and program. |
| Managed care organization (MCO) | An organization contracted to arrange or administer covered care under a managed-care model. | A plan coordinates defined services for enrolled members. | Contract responsibilities and payment arrangements vary. |
| Behavioral health MCO (BH-MCO) | An MCO responsible for a defined behavioral-health program or benefit. | A plan administers applicable services under its contract. | Do not assume every county, service, or person follows the same arrangement. |
| Eligibility | Whether someone meets program or benefit requirements at a particular time. | Enrollment is checked for the date of service. | Not the same as authorization, provider availability, or claim payment. |
| Benefit / covered service | A service included under applicable coverage rules, subject to conditions. | A type of outpatient service may be covered. | Verify the exact benefit and conditions in the governing source. |
| Network | Providers or organizations contracted or recognized by a plan for specified purposes. | A clinic participates in a plan's network. | A directory listing does not guarantee current participation or an open appointment. |
| Prior authorization | A review that may be required before certain services are provided or continued. | A plan reviews a request against applicable criteria. | Requirements, timelines, exceptions, and protections depend on governing rules. |
| Claim | A structured request for payment submitted by a provider or billing entity. | A clinic submits service details. | A claim may be pending, rejected, denied, paid, or adjusted; these states differ. |
| Encounter | A record of a service or contact, often used for reporting and oversight. | A plan reports a service delivered to a member. | Encounter data and payment transactions are related but not identical. |
| Billed charge | The amount a provider submits as its charge for a service. | A fictional claim shows $150. | Not automatically the amount paid or owed by the member. |
| Allowed amount | The amount recognized under applicable payment terms for a service. | A toy contract allows $90 from a $150 charge. | Actual calculation depends on contract, fee schedule, benefit, and circumstances. |
| Member responsibility | Amount the member is required to pay under applicable rules, if any. | A fictional plan assigns a copayment. | Do not infer it merely by subtracting payment from the billed charge. |
| Denial | A decision not to pay a claim or approve a request, for a stated reason. | Required information is missing or a rule is not met. | Check the actual reason, appeal options, and rights against applicable rules. |
| Appeal | A process to challenge a decision under applicable procedures. | A member or provider requests review. | Deadlines and procedures vary; consult official plan and program materials. |
| Capitation | A prospective payment for a defined population, service scope, and period, under contract terms. | A plan receives a set payment for a covered group over a month. | Not the same as paying a clinic for each service; contracts may include adjustments. |
| Administrative cost | Resources used to operate the system rather than directly deliver a specific clinical service. | Claims processing or required reporting. | Accounting definitions vary; state the framework before comparing totals. |
| Reimbursement | Payment under applicable rules or contract for a service or defined responsibility. | A payer pays a clinic for an approved service. | The term alone does not identify rate, timing, conditions, or payer. |
| Access to care | The practical ability to obtain appropriate care. | A person can find, reach, and attend an appropriate appointment. | Coverage alone does not ensure capacity, transportation, language access, or timely care. |

## Metaphors and limits
- **Insurance is not a magic pass.** Coverage helps pay for care, but access also depends on availability and applicable requirements.
- **A claim is not a cash transfer.** It is a request that must be processed.
- **A directory is not an appointment calendar.** Listing and capacity are different.
- **A budget is not a staffing roster.** Funds and available qualified clinicians are different.
- **A model is not a policy source.** SimSurance must point to source versions before presenting a rule as official.
